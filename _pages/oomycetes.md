Oomycetes are a diverse group of filamentous eukaryotic microorganisms found
in terrestrial, freshwater and marine environments. Although their filamentous
growth often resembles that of fungi, oomycetes belong to the stramenopiles
and are evolutionarily distant from true fungi.

## Oomycete biology

Oomycetes display remarkable diversity in their lifestyles and ecological
roles. They include saprophytes, parasites of plants and animals, and numerous
species associated with aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems.

Among the best-known oomycetes are species of *Phytophthora*, *Pythium*,
downy mildews and water moulds. Several are responsible for destructive
diseases of crops and natural ecosystems.

## Oomycete plant pathogens

Plant-pathogenic oomycetes have major agricultural and ecological importance.
Species such as *Phytophthora infestans*, the causal agent of potato late
blight, and numerous other *Phytophthora* species infect crops, forests and
natural plant communities worldwide.

Their biology combines rapid growth, sophisticated host interactions and
complex developmental transitions, making oomycetes important models for
studying both microbial cell biology and plant–microbe interactions.

## Molecular genetics and genomics

Modern oomycete research encompasses genomics, genetics, gene regulation,
cell biology, evolution, population biology and host–pathogen interactions.

Genomic sequencing and functional approaches have revealed rapidly evolving
effector repertoires, specialised secretion systems and signalling pathways
that contribute to development and pathogenicity.

Genome editing, live-cell imaging, transcriptomics, proteomics and
computational biology are increasingly expanding the experimental toolbox
available to the field.

## An international research community

The **Oomycete Molecular Genetics Network (OMGN)** brings together researchers
working across these areas of oomycete biology, fostering scientific exchange,
methodological innovation, international collaboration and support for
early-career researchers.
